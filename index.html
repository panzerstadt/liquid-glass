How this was made

A fragment shader draws the backdrop and every pane of glass. Each surface is a rounded-rect distance field: the interior is a flat plateau where displacement is exactly zero, and a fixed-width bevel at the rim does all the bending. The bevel profile is sin(πs)^curve, so the join into the plateau is soft instead of a crease — that is why nothing streaks down the middle of a long pill.

Frost is a flat plane, not a function of the shape. Constant kernel radius, constant mip level, sampled at the undisplaced coordinate: a 16-tap disc over a pre-blurred level. It also drains chroma (√frost·0.62 + 0.22·frost toward luma) and adds a scatter lift on the lit side, because real frosting greys and brightens as much as it blurs.

Two movable lights give the rim its gradient and glints; the highlight is tinted with the backdrop's own colour plus a small radial dispersion — offset from the optical centre, never a scaled coordinate, which is what used to paint rainbow edges.

The sheet's glass lives on a second canvas above the page, with a blur layer between the two, so it can frost the panel behind it without softening its own rim, bevels and handles.
